# ClimAID Policy Documentation

## Climate-Driven Multi-Disease Risk Assessment Framework for Public Health Planning

---

## 1. Purpose

ClimAID is a scientific decision-support framework designed to help policymakers and public health agencies assess how climate variability and climate change may influence infectious disease risk at the district level.

The framework translates complex climate projections into interpretable disease risk insights for evidence-based planning.

---

## 2. Policy Relevance in the Context of Climate Change

Climate change is altering key environmental drivers of infectious disease transmission, including:

* Temperature variability
* Rainfall patterns
* Humidity and atmospheric conditions

These environmental shifts significantly influence the transmission dynamics of climate-sensitive diseases, particularly vector-borne diseases such as dengue and malaria.

ClimAID enables forward-looking assessment of these risks under future climate scenarios.

---

## 3. Multi-Disease Risk Modelling

Unlike single-disease forecasting tools, ClimAID is designed as a generalized framework capable of modelling multiple climate-sensitive diseases using a unified methodological pipeline.

This supports:

* Comparative disease risk analysis
* Integrated public health planning
* Cross-disease vulnerability assessment

---

## 4. Climate Scenario-Based Decision Support (CMIP6)

ClimAID incorporates IPCC-aligned CMIP6 climate scenarios:

* SSP126 (Low emissions pathway)
* SSP245 (Intermediate pathway)
* SSP370 (High emissions pathway)
* SSP585 (Worst-case emissions scenario)

This allows policymakers to evaluate how disease risks may evolve under different climate futures, supporting climate adaptation strategies in the health sector.

---

## 5. Key Outputs for Public Health Agencies

* District-level disease risk projections
* Scenario-based future burden estimates
* Climate sensitivity insights
* Anomalous signal detection and risk assessment
* Visual dashboards for stakeholder communication
* Automated policy-style summary reports

---

## 6. Use Cases in Public Health Policy

* Climate-resilient health system planning
* Vector control prioritization
* Early warning system development
* Resource allocation to high-risk districts
* National and subnational climate-health adaptation planning

---

## 7. Offline and Secure Deployment

ClimAID is designed for:

* Government environments
* Secure research institutions
* Offline deployment without cloud dependencies
* Data sovereignty compliance

Local LLM integration enables automated reporting without external API usage.

---

## 8. Interpretation Guidelines for Policymakers

Important considerations:

* Projections are scenario-based, not deterministic forecasts
* Results reflect climate sensitivity, not exact outbreak counts
* Should complement surveillance data and epidemiological expertise
* Best suited for strategic planning rather than real-time outbreak prediction

---

## 9. Ethical and Responsible Use

ClimAID is intended to support:

* Evidence-based public health decision-making
* Climate adaptation policy development
* Long-term disease risk preparedness

It is not intended for:

* Clinical diagnosis
* Individual patient risk prediction
* Emergency outbreak surveillance replacement

---

## 10. Strategic Value for Climate-Health Governance

By integrating climate science, epidemiology, and reproducible modelling, ClimAID provides a scientifically grounded framework for anticipating and mitigating climate-driven infectious disease risks in a changing climate.

---
## 11. AI-Assisted Interpretation and Decision Support

### 11.1 Role of AI in Public Health Decision Making

ClimAID integrates artificial intelligence not only for predictive modelling but also for automated interpretation of climate-driven disease risks. This AI-assisted reporting layer helps translate complex modelling outputs into actionable insights for policymakers and public health stakeholders.

---

### 11.2 Automated Policy-Ready Reports

The framework includes an offline Large Language Model (LLM) module that generates:

* Climate-driven disease risk summaries
* Scenario-based disease projections under CMIP6 pathways
* District-level vulnerability narratives
* Policy-oriented interpretation of model diagnostics

These reports are automatically derived from validated model outputs and climate projections, ensuring consistency between analytical results and policy communication.

---

### 11.3 Offline and Secure AI Deployment

The AI reporting system operates fully offline using local LLMs, enabling deployment in secure environments such as:

* Government health departments
* National research institutes
* Public health surveillance units
* Climate policy planning agencies

This offline-first design ensures compliance with data security and sovereignty requirements while maintaining advanced AI interpretability capabilities.

---

### 11.4 Human-in-the-Loop Interpretation

The AI-generated narratives are designed to support, not replace, expert judgment. Policymakers and epidemiologists are encouraged to interpret AI-assisted reports alongside surveillance data, domain expertise, and local health system knowledge.

---

### 11.5 Strategic Value for Climate-Health Governance

By combining predictive modelling with AI-assisted interpretation, ClimAID bridges the gap between complex climate analytics and practical policy communication. This integrated approach supports long-term adaptation planning, resource prioritization, and climate-resilient health system development under uncertain future climate scenarios.
